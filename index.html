What can my Chromebook do?

Today I can: Design a fair test, try it on your own device, and use evidence to explain what your device can do.

Ohio 2022: CS.D.7.a

ReadWorked exampleYour responseChecks

Your path today

  1. Read the four short sections. Say the task and three criteria in your own words.
  2. In the typing lab, type the original sentence, make the edit, and check each trial.
  3. Read the worked example. Complete your evaluation in the response box.
  4. Answer the practice checks. Use the reading link in feedback if you need a retry.

READ 1 / 4 · L1-R1

A device is a tool with limits

A computing device takes information in, follows instructions, and produces a result. Your Chromebook is a computing device. A keyboard sends letters in; the screen shows letters back. A capability is something a device can do. A limit is something it cannot do well in a certain situation. A device can be good for one task and poor for another. Today we will test typing and editing short text. We will not guess how fast every Chromebook is from one result.

READ 2 / 4 · L1-R2

Make a test before you start

An evaluation is a judgment based on evidence. Evidence is something you observe or measure. First name a task: type and edit a short announcement. Next choose criteria. Criteria are rules for judging success. Our criteria are: all the words appear, a change appears, and a button responds. A trial is one attempt at a test. Repeat the same test three times to see whether it works consistently. Keep the words, button, and browser tab the same so the comparison is fair. If you change two things at once, you may not know which change affected the result.

READ 3 / 4 · L1-R3

Do the test and report honestly

The typing lab below runs on this page. It does not send what you type to anyone. For each trial, type: Our club meets today. Then change today to Friday. Choose Check trial. The page checks whether the final text is exactly Our club meets Friday. after ignoring spaces at the ends. Record whether you saw the text appear and whether the button responded. If the page gives feedback, the button responded. If you mistype, correct the text and try again. A typing mistake does not prove the device failed. Do not enter your name or private information.

READ 4 / 4 · L1-R4

Use evidence, then name a limit

A useful conclusion names the task and the evidence. For example: this device handled short text editing because the final sentence appeared in three trials. This does not show that it can edit a long video. We did not test video. If something is blocked by a school rule, record blocked rather than calling the device broken. Do not change school settings or try to bypass a block. Ask your teacher for help. An honest incomplete result is more useful than an invented success.

See the thinking · worked example

From facts to a supported claim

Mina plans to test typing a club announcement. Her criteria are readable text, a visible edit, and a responding Check trial button. She uses the same sentence three times. In trial 1 she types Frday, notices the missing i, and fixes it. All three corrected trials pass. She concludes that her device can handle this short text task. She adds: I have not tested pictures or video. Her evidence supports the small claim she made; it does not support every possible task.

Hands-on lab · your real device

Three trials, one fair test

Type Our club meets today. Then change today to Friday. The final sentence should be Our club meets Friday. Use the same page and sentence each time. Check whether words, the edit, and feedback appear. Record honest results; do not enter personal information.

Source: test instructions. A typo is a reason to correct and retry, not proof of device failure.

Choose the right hardware + software

READ 1 / 4 · L2-R1

Two parts must work together

Hardware is the physical part of a computing system, such as a keyboard, screen, microphone, or processor. A processor carries out instructions. Software is the set of instructions a device runs. A browser and a text editor are software. A microphone alone does not make an audio recording: it needs software that can record. Software alone cannot record your voice without a working microphone and permission to use it. A combination is a hardware choice and a software choice used together.

READ 2 / 4 · L2-R2

Start with the job

A requirement is something a task must have. For a short typed announcement, you need a way to enter letters, a readable screen, and an editor that changes text. For an audio message, you need a working microphone and software that records sound. Our cards below describe fictional school devices and tools. Treat the facts on the cards as the evidence. These cards do not describe all real Chromebooks. Do not buy or install anything. Select a combination that meets every requirement before considering extras.

READ 3 / 4 · L2-R3

Read the comparison cards

Device A, the Classroom Chromebook, has a keyboard and readable screen but no working microphone in this fictional case. Device B, the Media Chromebook, has a keyboard, readable screen, and working microphone. Text Pad is browser software that edits text but does not record audio. Voice Pad is fictional browser software that records audio with an allowed working microphone but does not edit typed announcements. Both devices can open either tool. For a typed announcement, Device A with Text Pad and Device B with Text Pad both work. For an audio message, only Device B with Voice Pad meets all the requirements. The other pairings miss a required part.

READ 4 / 4 · L2-R4

Weigh choices using evidence

A tradeoff is giving up one benefit to get another. If two combinations both meet your requirements, choose using another stated criterion. In this activity, Device A uses less battery for text work than Device B; this is a supplied fact about the fictional cards, not a measurement of your device. If saving battery matters for the typed announcement, choose Device A with Text Pad. A newer-looking device is not automatically better for every task. Explain the task, the hardware, the software, and why another combination would be less suitable.

See the thinking · worked example

From facts to a supported claim

Owen must record a 20-second announcement. He compares Device A + Voice Pad with Device B + Voice Pad. Voice Pad can record, but Device A has no working microphone. Device B has one. Owen chooses Device B + Voice Pad because it has both required parts. He rejects Device B + Text Pad because Text Pad cannot record audio. He has evaluated combinations, rather than judging hardware by itself.

    Solve problems one step at a time

    Today I can: Use observations and one-change tests to find a routine problem, choose a safe solution, and communicate it.

    Ohio 2022: CS.T.7.a

    ReadWorked exampleYour responseChecks

    Your path today

    1. Read the five-step process and the safe-test rules.
    2. In the lab, reproduce the symptom, check two possible causes, and read the log.
    3. Apply the supported solution. Repeat the original task to verify it.
    4. Write a help message and complete the practice checks.

    READ 1 / 4 · L3-R1

    Describe before changing

    Troubleshooting means finding and solving a problem in a planned way. A symptom is what you notice, such as a sentence not appearing. A possible cause is a reason it might happen, such as a paused practice editor. These are different: the symptom does not prove the cause. Use our five-step process: describe the symptom; list possible causes; test one cause at a time; choose and check a solution; communicate the result. A test is an action that helps you decide whether a possible cause matches the evidence.

    READ 2 / 4 · L3-R2

    Test safely and fairly

    Start with simple, reversible checks. Reversible means easy to undo. In our practice lab, look at the status and check whether typing is paused. Changing the practice pause control is safe. Deleting files or resetting a device would risk losing work and would not be a reasonable first step. Change only one thing, then try the original task again. If you change many things at once, you cannot tell which one helped. In a real classroom, ask the teacher about unfamiliar messages, permissions, or school blocks. Never bypass school controls.

    READ 3 / 4 · L3-R3

    Find the cause in our practice case

    The lab is a simulation, which means a simplified model of a situation. It is not controlling your real Chromebook settings. The symptom is that choosing Send sample does not add a sentence to the practice output. The lab starts with practice typing paused. Check pause status: the lab reports paused. That supports pause as a cause. Choose Resume practice typing, then Send sample again. If the sentence appears, the original task works. Checking pretend Wi-Fi reports connected; in this case the task is on the page and does not need a network. Pretend Wi-Fi is not the cause. Reload case resets only the simulation.

    READ 4 / 4 · L3-R4

    Compare solutions and tell someone

    Evaluate a solution by asking whether the evidence supports it, whether it is safe, and whether it solves the original task. Resuming practice typing is a better solution here than deleting files: the pause status points to the cause, resuming is reversible, and sending again checks the fix. A useful help message includes the symptom, tests and results, the chosen solution and reason, and the final result. If your test does not solve a real problem, report that honestly and ask the teacher. Do not claim success just because you tried something.

      READ 1 / 4 · L5-R1

      Review: evaluate a device

      An evaluation is a judgment supported by evidence. First choose a task and success criteria. Then plan a test, keep conditions consistent, repeat it, and record observations. A trial is one attempt. Our typing test uses three trials of editing Our club meets today. to Our club meets Friday. The criteria are visible words, a visible edit, and a responding Check trial button. A conclusion must fit the evidence. Success with a short sentence supports short text editing, not long video editing. A typo can be corrected; it does not prove a hardware failure.

      READ 2 / 4 · L5-R2

      Review: evaluate a combination

      Hardware means physical parts. Software means instructions such as a browser or editor. A task can need both. The fictional Device A has a keyboard and readable screen but no working microphone. Device B has all three. Text Pad edits text, not audio. Voice Pad records audio with an allowed working microphone, not typed announcements. A + Text Pad and B + Text Pad both fit a typed announcement. A uses less battery for text in the supplied fictional facts. B + Voice Pad is the only pairing that fits recording an audio message. Check all requirements before deciding.

      READ 3 / 4 · L5-R3

      Review: troubleshoot and communicate

      Troubleshooting follows five steps: describe the symptom, list possible causes, test one cause at a time, choose and check a solution, and communicate results. In our simulation, Send sample produces no sentence because practice typing is paused. Pretend Wi-Fi is connected. Resume practice typing is an evidence-supported, reversible solution. Repeat Send sample and look for the sentence to verify the fix. Deleting files risks work and is not justified by this evidence. A help message includes symptom, tests and results, solution and reason, and the final check.
